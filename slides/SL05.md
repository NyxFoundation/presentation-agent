# The distinction between 'theory' and 'practice' has dissolved—Ethereum research now appears in the same top venues where you publish.

- Groth16 (EUROCRYPT 2016): foundational pairing-based SNARK by Jens Groth, now deployed in zkSync and other L2 systems
- Aurora (EUROCRYPT 2019): transparent succinct arguments with O(log² n) proof size, by Ben-Sasson, Chiesa et al.
- Lattice-based SNARKs (CRYPTO 2022): first publicly-verifiable, post-quantum secure SNARK construction by Albrecht et al.
- This represents an opportunity for Japanese researchers to establish presence in a growing field

<!-- Speaker Notes:
If you have any doubt about the academic legitimacy of this work, consider the publication record. Jens Groth's 2016 EUROCRYPT paper on pairing-based arguments—now universally called 'Groth16'—has become the foundation for billions of dollars in deployed infrastructure. The Aurora proof system, published at EUROCRYPT 2019 by Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, and colleagues, introduced transparent arguments that are now standard components in blockchain scaling.

At CRYPTO 2022, Albrecht and colleagues presented the first lattice-based SNARK that is simultaneously post-quantum secure, publicly verifiable, and recursively composable. This is frontier cryptography, published in the field's top venues.

The researchers contributing to this ecosystem come from Stanford, MIT, Berkeley, EPFL—the same institutions whose work you already cite. The venues are the same. The rigor is the same. Only the application context is new.

I should note that the examples I've cited are primarily from Western researchers. This represents an opportunity rather than a barrier—Japanese researchers have the expertise to make significant contributions to this field, and the path to doing so is more accessible than it may appear.
