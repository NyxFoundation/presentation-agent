# Nyx Foundation provides the collaborative infrastructure to translate Japanese cryptographic expertise into Ethereum research contributions.

- Bridge function: connecting Japanese academic researchers with PSE and EF research networks
- Specific support: we address language barriers in technical discussions, unfamiliarity with Ethereum-specific terminology, and lack of warm connections to PSE researchers
- Practical services: warm introductions, grant application assistance, technical context translation
- Track record: contributed accepted vulnerability reports to major Ethereum clients including Geth and Prysm

<!-- Speaker Notes:
This is where Nyx Foundation enters the picture. You might reasonably ask: why do I need an intermediary? You're capable researchers who can read an RFP and submit a proposal.

The answer is that Japanese researchers face specific barriers that we're positioned to address. First, language barriers—not in reading papers, but in the real-time technical discussions where research directions get shaped. Second, unfamiliarity with Ethereum-specific terminology and context that can make grant applications less compelling even when the underlying research is excellent. Third, the lack of warm connections to PSE researchers who can provide context on which problems are most pressing.

We understand both contexts. We know the rhythms of Japanese academic life—the teaching loads, the administrative requirements, the publication pressures. And we have deep connections within the Ethereum research community, including PSE and the broader Ethereum Foundation ecosystem.

Our team has contributed accepted vulnerability reports to major Ethereum clients including Geth and Prysm. We bring technical credibility alongside cultural understanding.

We are not gatekeepers. We are connectors. Any researcher who wishes to engage directly with the Ethereum Foundation is welcome to do so. Our goal is simply to make that path easier to find.
